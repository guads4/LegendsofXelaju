## Mystery of the Immortal Elf

- For **humans and under-wellers**, the story goes that some elves can live thousands of years
  - It's very rare, and it's thought that maybe only a handful of non elves have ever met any of these ancient elves
  - Most think they are probably hermits, perhaps hiding from others or taking on alter egos throughout the ages
- The **elven** myth says that its just one elf thats ever been known to live past a thousand years old
  - No one has seen them in centuries nor even remembers their name
  - Most believe they aren't real, and that it came up as a way to mess with humans
  - Others believe they learned the secret to immortality and climbed up the Ceiba Tree to the celestial plane

## The Legend of the Hero Twins

> [!important] Ball is Life (and death)

One day, [[Pantheon#Hun-Came (hoon ka-me) - Lord of Xibalba|Hun-Came]] and [[Pantheon#Vucub-Came - God of Suffering|Vucub-Came]] invited renowned Pok Ta Pok (ballgame) players, Hun-Hunahpu and Vucub-Hunahpu, to Xibalba to play ball. They passed through the many horrors of Xibalba without harm. Once they arrived at the court of the Lords of Xibalba, all of the Xibalbans laughed until their insides hurt and their chests became cramped from their laughter. The Jaguar Gods offered the brothers a seat but it was nothing but a very hot stone. They were tricked and harmed and ultimately decapitated. Hun-Hunahpu's head was hung from a fruit tree. [[Pantheon#Ixquic - Goddess of the Waning Moon|Ixquic]], who had travelled to Cab during this time, passed by and caressed the head of Hun-Hunahpu.

Upon this gesture of kindness the head spoke, "[[Pantheon#Ixquic - Goddess of the Waning Moon|Lady Ixquic]], please allow my name to survive, to avenge this humiliation."

"Dear Hun-Hunahpu, kiss this hand that caresses you and I shall make it so."

And so Hun-Hunahpu kissed the palm of [[Pantheon#Ixquic - Goddess of the Waning Moon|Lady Ixquic]] and his spit impregnated her. Upon hearing this news, [[Pantheon#Hun-Came (hoon ka-me) - Lord of Xibalba|Hun-Came]] and [[Pantheon#Vucub-Came - God of Suffering|Vucub-Came]] were furious so she fled to [[Pantheon#Ixumcane and Ixpiyacoc - Goddesses of Love and Relationships|Hun-Hunahpu's mother's]] house. Here the twins Hunahpu and Ixbalanque were born. [[Pantheon#Ixumcane and Ixpiyacoc - Goddesses of Love and Relationships|Her Mother-in-laws]] knew that the lords of Xibalba would not take this treachery lightly so they sent [[Pantheon#Ixquic - Goddess of the Waning Moon|Ixquic]] away and refused to tell the boys the truth of their parentage.

At this time [[Pantheon#Ixumcane and Ixpiyacoc - Goddesses of Love and Relationships|Ixumcane and Ixpiyacoc]] were also caring for two orphan twins, [[Pantheon#Hun-Batz - Patron of the Arts|Hun-Batz]] and [[Pantheon#Hun-Chouen - Patron of the Arts|Hun-Chouen]]. These two boys were mischievous half howler monkeys. The four boys often played Pok Ta Pok together becoming very skilled ball players. One day, a rat made its way onto the field. The boys tried to chase him off but he claimed he had information about Hunahpu and Ixbalanque's parents. After feeding the rat he told them of their father's story. Filled with rage, Hunahpu and Ixbalanque pledged to avenge their father.

As the twins followed in their father and uncles footsteps, [[Pantheon#Hun-Came (hoon ka-me) - Lord of Xibalba|Hun-Came]] and [[Pantheon#Vucub-Came - God of Suffering|Vucub-Came]] laid the same tricks as the last time. This time however, the twins had gifts from [[Pantheon#Hun-Batz - Patron of the Arts|Hun-Batz]] and [[Pantheon#Hun-Chouen - Patron of the Arts|Hun-Chouen]], and out witted them at every turn. Time finally came to play Pok Ta Pok. In the last round, the Jaguar Gods taunted the twins, reminding them that if they do not win out right they will be executed. The twins were able to seal the deal in the final seconds. The Jaguar Gods in their fury tried to burn the twins in an oven. However, [[Pantheon#Ixchel - Godess of Midwifery, Medicine and the Whole Moon|Ixchel]], knowing this would be the outcome, was able to secretly save their remains to bring them back.

With the help of the mischievous [[Pantheon#Hun-Batz - Patron of the Arts|Hun-Batz]] and [[Pantheon#Hun-Chouen - Patron of the Arts|Hun-Chouen]], they disguised themselves as entertainers. They performed miracles and illusions. For their last trick, they pretended to kill and revive Ixbalanque. The Jaguar gods were so impressed they volunteered to experience that as well. The twins were happy to oblige, and with a magic weapon from their mother, killed [[Pantheon#Hun-Came (hoon ka-me) - Lord of Xibalba|Hun-Came]] and [[Pantheon#Vucub-Came - God of Suffering|Vucub-Came]]. With this victory the twins declared that the Jaguar Gods would no longer be allowed to freely harass the children of the [[Pantheon#Creator Gods|Creator Gods]].

## Coming of Age

- The concept of ages for under-dwellers and elves were not defined by specific numbers but by stage of life.
  - The stages being: infant, child, adolescent, adult, elder, ancestor
  - Traditionally, transitions from one stage to another were often accompanied by set of trials/challenges to ensure that the person has learned the lessons that were culturally accepted of their "stage" and were ready to move on.
    - Commemorated with party or festival (when celebrating many people at once)
    - Some trials emphasized teamwork and others were meant to show what you could do on your own
    - "Failing" to pass was not seen as a moral failure but as a way for the gods to show you what aspects you need to work on
- Since humans have much shorter lifespans, they mainly just celebrate when folks reach the adult phase
  - Generally humans earn this phase when they start a career and/or a family
    - Usually between the ages 18-21
  - The other stages are still important and taken into consideration by the community
    - Infants are cared for by the community until they develop the ability to begin problem solving
    - Children are taught independence and tools for navigate more complex problems
    - Adolescents begin to enter specialized fields as apprentices
      - Farming, hunting, medicine, etc.
      - In the later years of adolescent they start to be placed in leadership roles
    - Adults take on the role of mentor or master with their main job being to teach the younger generations
    - Elders are sources of wisdom
      - Many still choose to follow their craft to whatever extent they can
    - Ancestors are prayed to for guidance and wisdom
